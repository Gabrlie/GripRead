# TXT reader Batch 2: chapters and reading modes

Status: completed

## Outcome

Deliver the next batch of the [TXT reader task](2026-09-24-txt-reader-modes-and-settings.md): chapter navigation and progress, single-page and double-page reading, bounded continuous scrolling, and mode-appropriate effects and automatic reading. Repair the three Batch 1 review findings before extending their consumers. Acceptance targets the macOS app at desktop and narrow-window sizes.

Behavior belongs to [Reader](../subsystems/reader.md), [interaction design](../design/interaction.md), and [motion design](../design/motion.md). The [navigation and modes decision](../notes/implemented/2026-09-27-reader-navigation-and-modes.md) records the delivered shared contract.

Font/image assets, schema 9, complete typography, information bars, chapter page counts, battery state, and toolbar customization remain in later batches. EPUB, online sources, RSS, sync, AI, and Android/Windows acceptance remain outside this task.

## Acceptance criteria

- All three review findings are resolved. Cross-page search highlights remain visible; slow writes do not bypass the inactivity delay; panel close, leaving, and normal exit flush the latest settings; Contents retains its selection within the book session.
- Chapter ranges cover the body without overlaps or gaps, including preface, no headings, one heading, duplicate starts, and an empty book. A chapter seek at 0% reaches its start; 100% shows its end without changing the current chapter. Emoji at range/page/window boundaries never split. Previous/next chapter disable at the outer boundaries, and a slow or failed TOC build does not block readable text.
- A single page or spread never displays another chapter as a side effect of a 100% seek. Normal forward/backward reading still crosses chapters continuously. A spread is committed and saved once, never as two observable page turns. Odd chapter endings have no duplicated or invented right-page text.
- Switching between single-page, double-page, and scroll preserves the same passage. Test 899, 900, and 901 CSS px viewports, a narrow viewport, late font loading, and resizing during navigation. Responsive fallback does not overwrite the stored mode. Toolbar/panel visibility causes no reflow or locator change.
- Scroll prepend and recycling keep the anchored text at the same viewport position. Long paragraphs, blank lines, Chinese/English, and emoji cross block boundaries without missing/repeated text or extra paragraph spacing. Search and bookmarks restore the target passage; manual scroll is saved on pause/leave/exit and restored after process restart. A final chapter shorter than the viewport stays current at 0%, 100%, and after restart; trailing alignment space has no locator or progress contribution.
- Failed or delayed adjacent reads retain usable content and offer retry. Repeated seek/mode/reflow requests reject stale results. Applying a rule while a chapter-end seek is delayed invalidates the old directory generation before that seek can publish, retaining the current passage. Encoding changes cannot publish old text or chapter ranges. Selection remains usable while automatic reading is paused and blocks under selection are protected from recycling.
- Effects finish or cancel under rapid navigation, resize, mode changes, panel opening, and reduced motion. Automatic reading waits for page/spread completion, stops at the book end, pauses under all existing interruption conditions, and never resumes or catches up elapsed background time automatically. Exit drains its final anchor before completing persistence.
- For 1, 10, and 50 MiB fixtures, body windows, rendered blocks, and animation clones remain within the explicit controller budget regardless of book length. Verify bounds after sustained forward and backward reading, not just first render. Record first readable render, mode-switch/reflow time, and peak rendered content; no whole-book layout or re-import is permitted.

## Verification

Use deterministic frontend tests for chapter intervals, navigation ownership, one-save-per-spread, bounded windows, and automatic-reading pause races. Rust tests exercise old JSON defaults, invalid new settings, version-checked offset resolution, surrogate boundaries, and unchanged schema 8 data. Real WebView tests establish column geometry, scroll anchoring, text selection, and native restart/exit behavior; jsdom cannot establish those layout claims.

`pnpm check` passes with 136 frontend and 66 Rust tests, including formatting, documentation, generated bindings, dependency boundaries, both TypeScript projects, Rust static checks, and the production frontend build. `pnpm tauri build --debug` passes on macOS.

Native acceptance passes across isolated runs of core, failures, restart, instant/curl interaction, presentation, TXT tools, reader tools, reading modes, and crash recovery, including the expected injected publication crash. The new reader-modes phase covers chapter ends and restart, 899/900/901 px fallback, bidirectional long-paragraph recycling, large books, all spread effects, and automatic scroll pause. An initial run lost its embedded-driver connection; subsequent runs also exposed an occluded window with a suspended animation timeline. The harness now foregrounds its real test window before interactions/visible-layout reads. Assertions and production motion remain enabled; failed phases were rerun rather than counted as passes.

Native review fixed a hidden LF becoming the saved visible anchor, which reopened a short final chapter in the previous chapter. It also exposed WebKit rounding away sub-pixel automatic-scroll steps; carrying the fractional distance across frames restores the selected speed. Frontend regression tests additionally cover odd chapter endings when moving backward and retrying a failed mode change without losing the passage.

For already-imported 1, 10, and 50 MiB ASCII long-paragraph fixtures, first-page rendering plus switching to scroll took approximately 420, 810, and 2904 ms in the final native run. Each initially mounted 8192 UTF-16 units; sustained forward/backward recycling stayed within three windows. These measurements include service/UI/test-driver work with warm filesystem caches and exclude import latency. No full-book DOM is mounted.

Desktop double-page, responsive single-page, desktop/narrow scroll, and existing reader-control captures were inspected. The intended visual changes are the chapter progress row and mode layouts; existing paper surfaces and overlay controls remain. Reduced-motion selection and cancellation are covered through reader-view and adapter tests; the macOS system preference was not manually toggled for a separate native screenshot. Other-platform acceptance and the existing source-picker evidence remain outside this batch.

# Reader

Status: planned

## Scope

Parse TXT and DRM-free reflowable EPUB, render normalized chapters, and support offline reading, navigation, search, and bookmarks. Online chapters enter through the same content interface. Screen layout and default typography belong to [interaction design](../design/interaction.md). The current TXT mode work is tracked in the [active plan](../plans/2026-09-27-reader-batch-2.md); EPUB remains planned.

## Interfaces

`ReaderLocator` identifies a chapter or EPUB resource, text offset, and surrounding text context within a book's content version. It is independent of device page numbers. The library's `Book` identity links locators and bookmarks to the same work; a changed file or source content does not silently reuse an incompatible position.

Rust supplies normalized chapter content, resource references, text search, and persisted positions. The frontend reader measures WebView fonts and viewport dimensions, paginates normalized content, maps displayed passages to locators, and handles selection. Both sides use the same locator semantics. The M0 TXT implementation normalizes line endings to LF and measures offsets in UTF-16 code units, rejecting offsets inside a surrogate pair; its generated Rust-to-TypeScript declarations are the IPC source of truth.

Keep these interfaces sufficient for future read-only content retrieval; do not add embedding or generation services to the reading path. The M0 implementation exercises the serialization and offset rules through Rust tests and typed IPC; later EPUB/source resources must preserve the same locator contract.

## Behavior

TXT defaults to single-page reading, with double-page and continuous-scroll modes available. Paginated screens contain only one chapter; an odd final spread leaves its right column empty, and the next turn enters the next chapter. The selected double-page mode shows spreads at 900 CSS px and wider, or in landscape from 600 CSS px (phones turned sideways); narrower screens display one page without changing the saved preference. Instant, curl, slide, and fade effects operate on the complete visible page/spread; Instant, curl, slide, fade, and cover effects are available. Curl is a canvas renderer ported from Legado 3.26.7's SimulationPageDelegate, including its next-page, lifted-edge, and fold shadows; slide and cover move copies of the two screens built by the page's own text renderer, so glyphs and numerals keep their layout while moving. All three follow a dragging finger, and Motion owns fade. Swipes and side taps lift the corner nearest the touch; a drag that starts in the middle third begins level and folds toward the top or bottom corner as the finger moves down or up. The curl rasterizer draws each glyph inside its laid-out box (tabular digits centered, and after the space justification puts in front of a wide character that follows a narrow one such as a curly quote), and reader text keeps full-width punctuation so the raster matches the page. Like Legado's 底部对齐, a page full but for less than about a line spreads that space over its paragraph gaps, so the last line sits at the bottom; chapter first pages, whose heading is taller than a line, are the usual case. Gaps do not change wrapping, so pages and positions stay as measured. A page is measured once for it (the gap after a heading only grows past the heading's collapsing margin), and page-turn copies take the fill of the page on screen, so a turn lays out no extra copies. Adapters own neither content loading nor saved positions. Scroll retains the selected effect for returning to pagination. General interface transitions follow [motion design](../design/motion.md).

Changing fonts, size, orientation, viewport, or single/double-page layout reflows from the text locator; leaving scroll mode records the passage at the top of the scroll view when the new mode is chosen, while the scroll text is still laid out, and never measures the replaced layout. A width change while scrolling (for example a dialog's scroll lock) keeps the passage at the top instead of reporting the line the reflow pushed there. Toolbar visibility and panel changes overlay stable page geometry and do not repaginate or alter the saved passage. Cross-chapter turns must preserve content without missing or repeating text. Retain only the required reading window in the DOM; a large novel must not be rendered in full.

Load local content first. Missing resources produce actionable loading or failure states without discarding readable content. Persist position independently of animation completion. Selection temporarily disables page-turn gestures. Reader search and bookmarks use content positions, not display pages.

Selecting text shows Legado's text action menu once the selection settles: copy, bookmark, search, replace, and share. Replace opens the replace rule editor with the selected text as a plain pattern scoped to the book; saving applies it at once and turns the book's rules on if they were off. The selection maps to book offsets because rendered text keeps hidden line feeds and indents, so a bookmark made from it starts at the selected text and saves an optional note; notes are shown, searched, and edited in the Contents bookmark list. On Android the WebView's own selection toolbar is emptied while the reader is open, and share uses the system sheet. Search can treat the query as a regular expression matched within each line, as Legado does; an invalid expression reports an error, and a line that exceeds the backtracking limit is skipped.

While a book is open the reader applies the screen orientation, kept-on screen, hidden system bars, and volume keys, and restores them on leaving. Brightness from the reading menu lasts for that reading session only and returns to the system on leaving. Volume keys change the volume again while the reading menu or a tool is open or read-aloud is speaking. The bottom bar shows the tools chosen and ordered in a dialog opened from the top bar's 更多 menu (one to seven, Legado's six by default), so it stays reachable whatever the bar shows; 简繁转换 is its own dialog from the same menu. Tapping the title, or 更多 › 书籍详情, opens the book's detail page over the reader. 添加书签 and 更新目录 confirm their outcome in the app's floating toast. A style preset may use a background image for its day or night variant; the image covers the whole screen and is drawn into page-turn copies. Images unused by the saved styles are removed at startup, not when a debounced save may still name a new one. The reading menu can take the page colours.

Replace rules and simplified/traditional conversion rewrite a book's stored text through the re-decode path, as recorded in the [text transforms decision](../notes/implemented/2026-10-10-text-transforms.md); each book can turn its rules off. Rules are imported from an address, a file, or pasted Legado JSON through the 替换净化 page's More menu; the rules are listed first, those already present marked and left unchosen, and only the chosen ones are saved. Read-aloud speaks paragraph by paragraph from the visible page, highlights the paragraph being read and, where the engine reports progress (Android `onRangeStart`, Web Speech boundaries), the words being spoken, with CSS custom highlights so the text is not re-rendered. Pages turn without animation when the spoken words pass the page end; in scroll mode the view follows the reading and loads later text. It stops at the end of the book, on the sleep timer, or on request, and queues a batch ahead so Android keeps speaking in the background. The 朗读 sheet reports a missing engine or failed speech and opens the system speech settings. Speed, pitch, and voice are style settings.

Clean EPUB and source markup before rendering; scripts and application IPC are not exposed to imported content. Resolve book resources through the file/content adapter rather than arbitrary paths supplied by markup. Image loading and late font availability must not silently move the reading position. EPUB books are converted to text at import ([decision](../notes/implemented/2026-10-14-epub-as-text.md)): DRM-protected and fixed-layout books are refused with a message, the book's own directory replaces chapter rules, and illustrations take a page-width box capped at the page height before they load.

For TXT, chapters follow Legado 3.26.7 as recorded in the [TXT chapter rules decision](../notes/implemented/2026-10-10-legado-txt-chapter-rules.md): a shared table of regex rules (Legado's defaults plus user rules), automatic choice of the best-scoring enabled rule, or a rule selected per book; text before the first heading becomes a 前言 chapter and the book introduction; a heading with no body before the next is a volume when its title is named like one (卷, 部, 篇, 册, 集, Volume, Book); size splitting applies when no rule matches, and over-long chapters may be split per book. Changing rules or selections rebuilds only directory metadata: it does not rewrite the body, change book identity or content version, or invalidate bookmarks and search positions. Directory caches are keyed by content version and the resolved rule input, concurrent builds for the same key are deduplicated, and stale builds are rejected after a newer choice. Encoding changes continue through the established content-version and verified-position relocation path, then rebuild rule-derived metadata for the new version.

Directory generation must not rescan the entire TXT once for every matched chapter. Build chapter locations in work proportional to the text and matches, independently from first-page rendering and shelf usability; benchmark cold and warm uncached cases with dense headings to verify the improvement.

Derive chapter ranges from accepted TOC positions, including a preface and whole-text fallback. Current chapter follows the leading visible locator. Chapter progress seeks resolve exact UTF-16 offsets through Rust; 100% reads backward from the exclusive chapter end and displays the terminal page/spread without entering the next chapter. Progress stays at 100% while that end is visible. A directory generation change cancels old chapter-targeted requests and retains the current passage. TOC loading never blocks body rendering.

Scroll renders at most three adjacent bounded windows and allows one transient read. Prepend and eviction preserve a visible text anchor; text-node mappings exclude hidden LF separators from visible anchors, and window boundaries do not introduce paragraph breaks. Selection defers recycling selected text. A bounded non-content spacer at EOF permits short final chapters and late locators to align at the viewport top. The spacer has no locator or progress contribution. Save the first visible UTF-16 position periodically during scrolling and synchronize it before leaving or exit; pixel adjustments are temporary layout state only.

Automatic reading uses the same controller and session pause state across modes. The 自动翻页 tool starts it at once. From starting it until leaving it, a tap on the menu zone shows Legado's automatic panel instead of the reading menu: its state, the interval or scroll speed, pause or resume, the reading menu, and exit; it does not block the page and hides after a few seconds while turning runs. Showing or hiding the panel never pauses turning; a manual turn during the session restarts the countdown, and only the panel's pause, the reading menu, or leaving stops it. Paginated reading advances one page/spread per saved interval with the page animation chosen in 界面; continuous reading uses a separately saved CSS-pixel speed, with defaults and bounds owned by the Rust preference declarations and generated bindings. It waits for page/window completion before the next interval, never overlaps turns, and pauses for manual reading interaction, controls or panels, text selection, blur/background, content replacement, reflow, encoding changes, navigation failure, or the final visible text. It never resumes after reopening or replays elapsed background time. Explicit automatic scrolling remains available with reduced motion, while decorative transitions are disabled.

### Online books

An online book is read chapter by chapter, as recorded in the [chapter resources decision](../notes/implemented/2026-10-12-online-chapter-resources.md). Each chapter is a reader resource (`chapter-<n>`) whose text is its title line and the source's content; locators are offsets inside that chapter, and a chapter's content version derives from its address and title. Content windows of an online book name the neighbouring resources and the chapter's position and count. Near a chapter edge the controller reads and measures the neighbouring chapter ahead, so the turn does not wait; the backend keeps the last 20 transformed chapter texts in memory. In single and double page modes the controller turns from a chapter's last page into the next chapter and from its first page into the previous chapter's last page, paginating that chapter from its start as forward reading does (so its first page is never short); 上一章 and 下一章 open the neighbouring chapter's start. Scroll mode reads on across chapters: near a chapter's end the next chapter is read and shown below it (the previous one, whole, above its start), the chapter holding the top of the view is the current one, and moving into a neighbour keeps the visible passage in place; positions are saved in that chapter. Read-aloud and automatic scrolling continue into the next chapter. Book progress is the chapter's share of the contents. Replace rules and simplified/traditional conversion apply each time a chapter is read, so changing them never fetches again. The contents list every chapter at its start, mark the chapter being read, and hide the TXT rule, split, and length options; TXT目录规则 and 设置编码 are hidden too. In-book search covers every chapter kept on the device, names each result's chapter by its resource and highlights a match only in its chapter, and says that uncached chapters are not searched. 上一章 and 下一章 stay available at a chapter's edge while a neighbouring chapter exists. The top bar has 缓存 for an online book, starting from the chapter on screen. After 换源 the reader forgets the old position, including a save not yet written, and opens the new source's matching chapter. Leaving a book that was opened from search without being added asks whether to 加入书架; declining keeps it off the shelf with its position and cache, so opening it from search again resumes there, and such books unopened for 30 days are deleted at startup.

## M0 implementation contract

TXT is one resource named Full text; content windows do not create chapters. Forward windows begin at their locator; backward windows end exclusively at it. Both reject surrogate-pair splits. Paginated layout measures each column using actual WebView typography and dimensions. Chapter slices preserve absolute offsets and whole-book length. Previous-page/spread navigation after restart reads backward instead of relying on session history.

Navigation is serialized within a session; seeks invalidate stale targets, and reflow queues behind accepted navigation. Complete spreads publish and save once. The application owns ordered/coalesced position writes across reader views; returning to the shelf cancels unshown navigation and immediately leaves while accepted writes continue. Reopening uses the latest session locator before a slow write finishes; encoding replacement clears that cache and uses the relocated backend position. Save failures retain their value and offer non-modal retry. Explicit save, native exit, and the document becoming hidden (Android pause) drain pending writes; exit ends animation without waiting for its duration. Reduced motion uses instant presentation, and selection, controls, and dialogs suppress reading gestures.

Supported preferences use the settings service and persist in SQLite, as recorded in the [preferences and assets decision](../notes/implemented/2026-09-24-reader-preferences-v2-and-assets.md). Reading colours come from day/night style presets and follow the application theme, which also follows live system changes when selected. Typography, margins, header and footer information, chapter title styling, and imported fonts change pagination, so the measurer re-measures from the current locator; a paragraph's own leading spaces are hidden in favour of the configured indent without changing text offsets. Reading uses bundled or imported fonts, local SVG icons, and local cover placeholders without remote asset requests.

## Acceptance

- Read a million-character TXT and an illustrated EPUB without mounting the entire book in the DOM.
- Build TXT directories with every default rule family, prefaces, volumes, size splitting, long-chapter splitting, custom and invalid rules; per-book rule changes preserve body text, content identity, and reader positions.
- Verify directory cache invalidation and stale-result rejection after rapid rule changes or encoding correction, and show that TOC work no longer performs a full-text scan per chapter.
- Turn across chapter boundaries in every reading mode without dropped or duplicated text.
- Run automatic turning at its default and a changed interval, verify each pause condition, and confirm reopening never starts it automatically.
- Change typography, orientation, window size, and page count, then reopen at the same passage.
- Exercise touch, keyboard navigation, selection, search, bookmarks, missing images, malformed content, and offline reopening.
- Replace the content version and verify position recovery reports uncertainty rather than silently selecting an unrelated passage.
